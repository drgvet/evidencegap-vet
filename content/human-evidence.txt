#
# analogy: close | partial | poor | none
#   close   - same drug class, same disease mechanism
#   partial - same class, different setting
#   poor    - the human question is only loosely the same
#   none    - no human analogue exists
#
# 'bears_on' lists the gap-map questions this class speaks to,
# separated by ·  — the titles must match the map exactly.

== Oral inotropes
key: inotropes
analogy: partial
trials:
  Two large placebo-controlled trials tested oral phosphodiesterase-3 inhibition
  in chronic heart failure and both were stopped for harm. Milrinone increased all-cause
  mortality by 28% in 1,088 patients with severe heart failure. Vesnarinone increased
  mortality dose-dependently in 3,833 patients, 22.9% against 18.9%, while
  *improving* quality-of-life scores in the same trial. The excess deaths in both
  were predominantly sudden.
transfer:
  Milrinone and vesnarinone are phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitors alone. Pimobendan
  combines phosphodiesterase-3 inhibition with calcium sensitisation, which is argued to
  raise contractility with a smaller rise in intracellular calcium and myocardial oxygen
  demand. The trial populations differ as well: those patients had predominantly ischaemic
  systolic dysfunction, in which the arrhythmic substrate thought to drive the excess
  mortality is already present. Myxomatous mitral valve disease is a volume-overload lesion
  in a non-ischaemic ventricle.
  Those differences are real and they may well be sufficient, but they do not dispose of the
  human result. The mechanism of harm was never established in the human trials, so the
  mechanistic argument for exemption remains an assertion rather than a finding. Neither EPIC
  nor QUEST was designed to detect a mortality difference of that size, since death was one
  component of a composite primary endpoint in both trials.
bears_on: Pimobendan in preclinical stage B2 disease · Pimobendan in congestive heart failure · Pimobendan in preclinical dilated cardiomyopathy (Dobermanns) · Pimobendan in feline congestive heart failure · Does preclinical pimobendan shorten survival once heart failure develops?

== Renin--angiotensin--aldosterone blockade
key: raas
analogy: close
trials:
  This is the best-populated area of the comparison. Enalapril in 4,228 asymptomatic patients
  with an ejection fraction of 35% or less cut the combined incidence of death or heart
  failure by 29%, and heart-failure hospitalisation by 20%, but **did not reduce
  mortality** (8% risk reduction, p = 0.30). Spironolactone
  reduced mortality by 30% in severe heart failure, 35% against 46%, and eplerenone
  reproduced the effect in mild disease. Sacubitril--valsartan reduced cardiovascular
  death or heart-failure hospitalisation by 20% and all-cause death by 16% against an active
  comparator in 8,442 patients.
transfer:
  Neurohormonal activation is the shared final pathway, and the analogy holds better
  here than anywhere else on this page. The analogy is not exact, because the human trials
  enrolled patients with reduced ejection fraction of ischaemic or idiopathic origin, whereas
  canine heart failure is usually valvular with systolic function preserved until late.
  Read together, the two literatures say the same thing. Blockade before symptoms alters
  progression at best and has never shown a survival benefit in either species, which is
  what SVEP and VETPROOF found in dogs and what SOLVD-Prevention found in people. Blockade
  after symptoms appear is where benefit has been demonstrated. The difference between the two literatures
  is one of strength rather than of direction, since the canine spironolactone result rests on
  39 events and the human one on 1,663 patients.
bears_on: ACE inhibitors before heart failure · Spironolactone with benazepril to delay the onset of heart failure · Spironolactone added to standard heart-failure therapy · Sacubitril--valsartan (ARNi) in congestive heart failure
---

== Loop diuretics
key: diuretics
analogy: close
trials:
  Torsemide and furosemide were compared head to head in 2,859 patients discharged
  after a heart-failure hospitalisation, with no difference in all-cause mortality
  (HR 1.02). Dose and route have been tested once, in hospital: DOSE randomised 308
  patients with acute decompensated heart failure to high-dose against low-dose intravenous
  furosemide and to bolus against continuous infusion, and both co-primary endpoints were
  null: no difference in symptom relief or creatinine for bolus versus infusion, and
  only a nonsignificant trend favouring the high dose (P = 0.06). Outpatient dose
  and dosing interval, which is what most prescribing actually is, have not been settled by
  trial in either species.
transfer:
  Both species are being asked the same pharmacological question about the same class
  of drug acting on the same segment of the nephron, and the answers agree with each other, in that the
  veterinary non-inferiority result says what a trial ten times larger says in people. The
  gap in dosing evidence is shared between the two fields rather than specific to veterinary
  medicine, which is worth stating plainly rather than treating as a failure peculiar to this
  field.
bears_on: Torasemide as an alternative to furosemide (dogs) · Furosemide dose and dosing frequency in stage C disease
---

== Suppression of ventricular ectopy
key: ectopy
analogy: partial
trials:
  CAST randomised 1,727 patients with asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic ectopy
  after myocardial infarction to encainide, flecainide or placebo. The drugs suppressed
  ectopy as intended. Total mortality was nonetheless 7.7% against 3.0%, a relative risk of
  about 2.5, and the trial was stopped early. No intermediate measure identified the harm,
  because the Holter recordings improved throughout.
transfer:
  At the level of the drug the analogy is weak. Encainide and flecainide are class Ic sodium
  channel blockers acting on scarred post-infarct myocardium, and the proarrhythmia is
  attributed to conduction slowing in that substrate. Sotalol and mexiletine belong to other
  classes, and arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy in the Boxer is a different substrate again.
  At the level of method the analogy is exact, and that is the part which transfers. Ectopy
  count is a surrogate outcome, it was suppressed, it moved in the intended direction, and
  the patients nonetheless did worse. Any protocol judged on Holter suppression, in any species, is exposed
  to the same failure, and no amount of suppression can serve as evidence of benefit on its
  own.
bears_on: When to treat ventricular arrhythmias in asymptomatic dogs · Sotalol versus mexiletine--atenolol in Boxer arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy · Holter thresholds that predict sudden death
---

== Beta blockade in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
key: beta
analogy: partial
trials:
  In obstructive disease, metoprolol reduced outflow gradients and improved symptoms
  against placebo, and remains a comparator in current trials. In **non-obstructive**
  disease a placebo-controlled triple-crossover trial found the opposite, in that bisoprolol
  reduced exercise capacity and quality of life while increasing NT-proBNP and left atrial
  size. No trial in either phenotype has tested a hard outcome.
transfer:
  The diseases are not equivalent. Human hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is a sarcomeric
  genetic disorder with an established mutation in around half of patients; feline
  hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is heterogeneous and, outside two breed-specific mutations, of
  unknown genotype.
  The distinction that does carry across is haemodynamic rather than genetic. Beta
  blockade helps the obstructive phenotype by slowing the heart and reducing the gradient, and
  it appears to harm the non-obstructive phenotype by limiting the rate response on which a
  stiff ventricle depends. Most cats given atenolol are subclinical and non-obstructive, which is the
  phenotype in which the human evidence is negative rather than merely absent.
bears_on: Atenolol in preclinical hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
---

== Antithrombotic therapy
key: antithrombotic
analogy: poor
trials:
  Clopidogrel with aspirin was compared directly with oral anticoagulation for stroke
  prevention in atrial fibrillation and was inferior, the trial stopping early
  (RR 1.44 favouring anticoagulation). Across the wider trial literature, antiplatelet
  therapy reduced stroke by about 22% and adjusted-dose warfarin by about 64%. In atrial
  fibrillation, antiplatelet therapy is inferior to oral anticoagulation and is not
  considered an equivalent substitute for it where anticoagulation is indicated.
transfer:
  This is the weakest analogy on the page. The human evidence concerns cerebral
  embolism from the left atrial appendage in atrial fibrillation. Feline arterial
  thromboembolism is usually distal aortic, arises from an atrium enlarged by cardiomyopathy,
  frequently in sinus rhythm, and occurs in a species in which anticoagulant monitoring is
  impractical and the bleeding trade-off is therefore different.
  What transfers here is a question rather than an answer. The comparison that settled the
  human field, which set antiplatelet therapy against anticoagulation, has not been made in
  cats. FAT CAT compared clopidogrel with aspirin, and that is a comparison between two
  members of the weaker class.
bears_on: Clopidogrel to prevent recurrent arterial thromboembolism
---

== Rate control in atrial fibrillation
key: rate
analogy: partial
trials:
  Lenient rate control, defined as a resting rate below 110 bpm, was not inferior
  to strict control for a composite of cardiovascular events in 614 patients, and was far
  easier to achieve.
transfer:
  The arrhythmia is the same in both species, and the setting is not. Human atrial fibrillation is
  frequently lone or hypertensive with preserved ventricular function, whereas canine atrial
  fibrillation usually accompanies advanced structural disease at much higher rates. The
  targets are not interchangeable either, being derived from Holter averages in dogs and
  resting rate in people.
  What survives translation is the burden of proof. Aggressive rate targets were assumed to
  be better until the assumption was tested, at which point it did not hold.
bears_on: Rate control in atrial fibrillation: diltiazem with digoxin versus monotherapy
---

== Sodium restriction and exercise
key: sodium
analogy: partial
trials:
  Both have been tested in humans and neither supports restriction. Reducing dietary
  sodium below 100 mmol per day made no difference to death or hospitalisation
  (HR 0.89, not significant) in 806 randomised patients. Supervised aerobic training in
  2,331 patients with chronic heart failure missed its primary endpoint
  (HR 0.93, p = 0.13) and reached significance only after adjustment for
  prespecified prognostic variables; it was safe in advanced disease.
transfer:
  Sodium intake is not comparable between a free-living human adding salt at table and
  a dog eating a formulated diet, and the achievable range of restriction differs
  accordingly. Exercise advice needs to be separated in the same way. Where restriction is advised in a
  dog with an arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy the aim is to prevent sudden death during
  exertion, which is a different question from functional capacity in stable heart failure and
  one that the human exercise trials do not address.
  In the ordinary case, meaning a dog with stable treated heart failure that is told to stay
  quiet and fed a sodium-restricted diet, neither practice has a trial behind it in either
  species, and the human trials that do exist are either neutral or point the other way.
bears_on: Dietary sodium restriction · Exercise restriction after diagnosis

== Glucocorticoids
key: steroids
analogy: partial
trials:
  Two separate literatures exist here, and they point in different directions. **Acute exposure:** a
  self-controlled case series of 2,623,327 adults given a single oral burst of fourteen days or
  less found a heart-failure incidence rate ratio of **2.37 (2.13--2.63) at days 5 to
  30**, attenuating thereafter, at an absolute rate of 1.3 per 1000 person-years. The commonest
  indications were skin disorders and respiratory infections. **Therapeutic use:** prednisone
  is given deliberately in heart failure to augment diuresis, and a randomised pilot in acute
  heart failure with raised C-reactive protein reported fewer events at 90 days (HR 0.31).
  **The mechanism has been measured.** Six normal subjects given five days each of
  prednisolone, **methylprednisolone**, **triamcinolone** and dexamethasone showed no weight
  gain, *increased* urinary sodium excretion, and plasma volume unchanged on radiolabelled
  albumin. The volume effect belongs to cortisol, not to the synthetics, although
  methylprednisolone *pulses* do suppress urinary sodium for up to 32 hours, with escape.
transfer:
  The acute association is the closest human analogue to the feline claim, and it is
  confounded in exactly the same way, because people receive steroid bursts on account of being
  acutely unwell, and respiratory infection is itself a common precipitant of decompensation.
  The drug and the precipitant arrive together, just as illness, handling and stress arrive
  together in the cat.
  The one population where that confounding was tested directly settles it as far as human data
  can. Among 11,356 patients in acute heart failure, those newly started on a corticosteroid had
  worse crude outcomes "essentially because corticosteroid-treated patients were
  sicker", and the difference disappeared on adjustment. Where allocation is random, nothing
  appears: 584 patients randomised to methylprednisolone or placebo for severe pneumonia had
  cardiovascular complications, on a definition explicitly including new or worsening
  congestive heart failure, in 4% against 5% by day 28.
  The transfer is therefore partial in a specific way. The *mechanism* proposed in cats is not
  supported, because plasma volume was measured after the same drug classes used in cats and
  did not change. The *association* transfers completely, and it carries its confounding
  with it.
bears_on: Do glucocorticoids precipitate congestive heart failure?
---

== mTOR inhibition
key: mtor
analogy: none
trials:
  There is no human trial of rapamycin or an analogue in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
  The randomised human data concern everolimus and drug-induced left ventricular hypertrophy
  in transplant recipients, where the hypertrophic stimulus is a calcineurin inhibitor that
  is being withdrawn at the same time. The results are small and mixed and the design
  confounds the question.
transfer:
  There is nothing here to extrapolate from in either direction. This is the one question on
  the map on which the veterinary evidence leads the human literature, which is a statement about human
  medicine and not an endorsement of the veterinary trial. A single 43-cat study reporting an
  effect below the measurement noise of its own endpoint is weak evidence whether or not
  anyone else has done better.
bears_on: Rapamycin (sirolimus) in subclinical hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
